Section 01 • The Root Cause

Why First REIT Broke: The "Illusion of Currency Immunity" (2006–2019)

Every problem First REIT faced stems from the original contract architecture created at its December 2006 IPO [1][6].

Flaw #1: Artificial SGD Master Leases Over IDR Cash Flows

Currency Mismatch

First REIT’s hospitals in Indonesia (operated by Siloam Hospitals Group) treated local Indonesian patients and collected revenue in Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). Yet, to market First REIT to Singapore retail investors as a "currency-hedged" high-yield play, the 15-year Master Lease Agreements (MLAs) signed with sponsor PT Lippo Karawaci Tbk (LPKR) required rent to be paid in Singapore Dollars (SGD) [4][6]. As SGDIDR=X surged from 5,854 to 10,891+, the local IDR cost of paying SGD rent outgrew what the hospitals generated for LPKR.

Flaw #2: Extreme Sponsor & Counterparty Concentration

Single-Point Failure

Instead of leasing directly to diversified healthcare operators at market-clearing rents, First REIT relied on LPKR (and Lippo-linked affiliate PT Metropolis Propertindo Utama [MPU]) for over 80% of its total rental income [4][8]. When LPKR suffered severe cash-flow burn from its Indonesian township developments (Meikarta) and COVID-19 disruptions in 2019–2020, LPKR could no longer subsidize the above-market SGD hospital rents [6].

Flaw #3: The 2018 Sponsor Split & Misaligned Incentives

Governance Shift

In September–October 2018, a cash-strapped LPKR sold its 100% stake in First REIT’s manager (Bowsprit Capital Corporation Limited) to OUE Limited (60%) and OUE Lippo Healthcare (40%), alongside a 10.63% stake in First REIT [3]. Once LPKR had cashed out of the REIT Manager, it had vastly diminished incentive to keep paying punitive SGD-pegged master lease rents to a REIT it no longer controlled [4][6].

Section 04 • Post-Restructuring Headwinds (2022–2025)

Why "First REIT 2.0" Still Saw Falling DPU From 2022 to 2025

01. The Double Currency Trap (IDR + JPY Depreciation)

The 2021 MLA restructuring shifted Indonesian hospital base rents from SGD into IDR (with a 4.5% annual escalation) [4], while First REIT 2.0 acquired 14 nursing homes in Japan collecting rent in JPY [7]. Between 2022 and 2025, a surging Singapore Dollar against both the IDR and JPY erased local-currency rental growth (in FY2025, Indonesian local rent grew +5.1%, yet SGD DPU fell -8.1% to 2.17¢) [9].

02. Chronic Rental Arrears from Lippo Affiliate MPU

Indonesian tenant risk persisted through PT Metropolis Propertindo Utama (MPU)—a Lippo-linked master lessee operating several malls and hospitals—which accrued ~S$7.9M of arrears in Sep 2024 and IDR 89.25 billion (~S$6.9M) at end-2025 (~S$7.68M in mid-2026) [8][10].

03. Higher Interest Rates & 42.1% Gearing Ceiling

Rising interest costs combined with FX translation write-downs on Indonesian and Japanese property values pushed First REIT’s aggregate leverage to 42.1% by early 2026, constraining debt headroom [10].

04. Unit Base Expansion via Management Fees

Paying REIT management fees in new units while the unit price traded at a steep discount to NAV (~S$0.176–S$0.27) expanded the unit count every quarter, diluting DPU further [9].

Section 05 • The 2026 Endgame & Visual Transformation

Cutting the Gordian Knot: The S$766.3M Complete Exit from Indonesia

Following a January 2025 LOI from PT Siloam International Hospitals Tbk (backed by CVC Capital Partners) and a Citigroup-led Strategic Review, First REIT announced definitive agreements on April 1, 2026 (approved at the June 2026 EGM and completing Tranche 1 in late Q3 2026) to exit Indonesia [10][12][13].
